# Cherry Blossom Bloom Dynamics: Project Summary

For a compact continuation guide, start with [next_chat_handoff.md](next_chat_handoff.md). It contains the current adopted model, outputs, commands and remaining work.

## Why We Chose This Problem

Cherry blossom timing gives us a visible biological record of climate. A bloom date is easy to understand, but it also carries information about temperature, geography and long-term environmental change. We chose this project to study two related questions:

1. How has the timing of cherry blossom flowering changed through time?
2. Can a simple dynamical or temperature-based equation explain that change?

## What Has Been Done

### 1. Kyoto historical case study

- Bloom timing changes by **−2.99 days per degree Celsius**; 95% CI **[−3.39, −2.60]**.
- The observed-temperature regression has $R^2 = 0.6623$.
- The post-1850 trend is **−7.53 days per century**; 95% CI **[−9.16, −5.89]**.
- The pre-1850 trend is small and later: **+0.31 days per century**; 95% CI **[+0.12, +0.50]**.
- The post-1950 trend is **−7.93 days per century**, with an overlapping confidence interval.

The main Kyoto figures are [step1_doy_vs_year.png](../plots/step1_doy_vs_year.png), [doy_trends_split.png](../plots/doy_trends_split.png), [residuals_vs_year_observed.png](../plots/residuals_vs_year_observed.png), and the observed-only temperature figure [kyoto_observed_doy_vs_temperature.png](../plots/kyoto_observed_doy_vs_temperature.png). The histogram [step1_doy_distribution.png](../plots/step1_doy_distribution.png) shows the overall distribution of Kyoto full-bloom dates.

### 2. JMA station trends

The main station analysis excludes Taiwan cherry, Kurile cherry and Kutchan, whose observed species changed. The cleaned histogram contains **90 stations**: 80 stations labelled Not stated and 10 Sargent cherry stations.

The cleaned station trend figure is [histogram_full_trends.png](../plots/histogram_full_trends.png), generated by [jma_trend_histogram.py](../scripts/jma_trend_histogram.py).

The 1961-2010 common-window analysis retains 73 Not stated stations and 8 Sargent stations, requiring at least 40 valid years. In that window:

- **98.6%** of Not stated stations have a negative full-bloom trend.
- **87.5%** of Sargent stations have a negative full-bloom trend.
- Not stated full-bloom trend median: **−1.27 days/decade**, IQR **[−1.58, −0.96]**.
- Sargent full-bloom trend median: **−1.09 days/decade**, IQR **[−1.44, −0.81]**.
- Not stated duration trend median: **+0.30 days/decade**.
- Sargent duration trend median: **−0.21 days/decade**.

### 3. Duration and regional structure

The national duration median shows geographic pattern. Some northern stations show shortening, while many southern and central stations show lengthening.

For 73 Yoshino-group stations in the common window:

- Mean full-bloom date becomes later by **3.541 days per degree latitude**; $R^2 = 0.873$.
- The full-bloom trend becomes more negative with latitude: **−0.062 days/decade per degree**; $R^2 = 0.115$.
- The duration trend becomes more negative with latitude: **−0.104 days/decade per degree**, 95% CI **[−0.134, −0.073]**, $R^2 = 0.395$.

The result means latitude is strongly related to the average bloom date and is also related to the direction of duration change. It does not by itself prove that latitude causes the trend. Latitude may represent several linked factors, including winter temperature, chilling conditions, elevation, coastal influence and station history. The results and figure are produced by [jma_latitude_analysis.py](../scripts/jma_latitude_analysis.py) and [latitude_regressions.png](../plots/latitude_regressions.png).

### 4. Temperature sensitivity across stations

Monthly February and March temperatures were downloaded for eight stations using the corrected JMA coordinates.

The station analysis compares full-bloom date with March temperature and duration with the February-March mean temperature. The figure is [temperature_sensitivity.png](../plots/temperature_sensitivity.png), generated by [jma_temperature_sensitivity.py](../scripts/jma_temperature_sensitivity.py).

The preliminary pattern is consistent with the duration results:

- Kyoto duration sensitivity: **+1.41 days per degree Celsius**, $R^2 = 0.40$.
- Fukuoka: **+1.55 days per degree Celsius**.
- Kagoshima: **+1.06 days per degree Celsius**.
- Osaka: **+1.20 days per degree Celsius**.
- Aomori: **−0.14 days per degree Celsius**, $R^2 = 0.01$.
- Asahikawa: **−0.14 days per degree Celsius**, $R^2 = 0.02$.
- Yamagata: **−0.07 days per degree Celsius**, approximately no relationship.

### 5. Physical equation attempt

A simple 400-degree thermal-sum rule was tested for Kyoto using daily temperatures. Positive daily temperatures were accumulated from February until the total reached 400 degree-days. A linear Feb-March temperature model was used as a comparison.

The figure is [step4_physical_equation.png](../plots/step4_physical_equation.png).

- 400-degree rule RMSE: **4.84 days**.
- Linear Feb-March model RMSE: **3.24 days** on the fitted comparison data.

The thermal-sum model is scientifically meaningful because it represents accumulated heat rather than only a seasonal average. However, the current implementation is a first baseline, not a validated physical model. The starting date, threshold temperature, degree-day threshold and chilling requirement all need calibration. The comparison also needs a clearly defined out-of-sample evaluation before claiming predictive superiority.

## What the Work Means

The strongest conclusion is that cherry blossom timing has advanced across Japan, and Kyoto's long record shows that the modern change is especially clear after the nineteenth century. The observed-temperature Kyoto analysis supports a strong relationship between warmer March conditions and earlier flowering.

The station results add an important detail. Bloom dates are nationally consistent, but bloom duration is not. A near-zero national median does not mean duration is unchanged everywhere. Opposing regional changes can cancel in a national summary. The latitude and temperature analyses suggest that warmer southern and central regions may experience a longer interval between first and full bloom, while northern stations more often show compression.

This connects three scales of evidence:

- Kyoto gives a long historical climate-sensitive record.
- The JMA stations show spatial consistency across Japan.
- The temperature and physical-model analyses begin to connect the observed dates to mechanisms.

The study does not establish that temperature is the only cause, and the station trends are not 102 independent experiments because nearby stations share weather and local climate.

## What Is Left To Do

1. **Improve the temperature analysis.** Download JMA monthly temperatures, or a consistent gridded product, for more stations representing both duration groups. Use the same station coordinates and document the source clearly.
2. **Test the duration mechanism directly.** Fit a regional or hierarchical model for duration against temperature, latitude, elevation and year. Report uncertainty and account for shared regional weather.
3. **Use a stronger common-window design.** Repeat station trends over several windows, such as 1961-2010 and 1971-2020, to test sensitivity to station start and end dates.
4. **Complete the Taiwan cherry analysis separately.** Season-relative dates from January 1 are appropriate, but Taiwan cherry should not be pooled with Yoshino or Sargent cherry.
5. **Improve the physical model.** Compare 400-degree and 600-degree rules, test different start dates and base temperatures, include chilling, and evaluate predictions out of sample.
6. **Check source overlap.** Confirm whether recent Aono Kyoto observations overlap the JMA Kyoto station series and avoid treating overlapping observations as independent.
7. **Confirm species labels.** Verify what Not stated means in the Kaggle/JMA source description rather than relying on the Yoshino assumption.
8. **Add robustness checks.** Consider non-linear temperature effects, robust regression, clustered or spatially correlated uncertainty, and corrections for multiple station comparisons.
9. **Create one final pipeline.** Combine the cleaned Kyoto, station, coordinate, temperature and plotting steps into a documented reproducible workflow. Keep exploratory scripts clearly marked as legacy.
10. **Prepare the final report.** Use the observed-only Kyoto temperature figure, the cleaned station histogram, the latitude figure and the temperature-duration figure. Do not use the original mixed reconstructed/observed temperature plots.

## Adopted Simple Model

The current final model is the empirical JMA temperature-response equation:

$$
D_{Kyoto} = 119.55 - 2.878T_{March}
$$

It uses 72 Kyoto JMA station-weather years, with $R^2 = 0.720$ and slope 95% CI **[−3.305, −2.450]** days/$^\circ$C. It is the simplest defensible model for this project: an empirical physical response, not a complete biological law. Degree-day and moving-equilibrium models remain exploratory comparisons.
